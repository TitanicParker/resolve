## Part I — Original sentences

### Corpus A — Statements about the world (A-001–A-100)

### A-001

**Source ID:** A-001  
**Original sentence:** The morning sun reaches mountain villages long before their valleys receive warmth.  
**What changed:** The receipt of morning sunlight and warmth becomes intelligibly differentiated across two connected locations.  
**What made it sufficient:** The temporal comparison establishes that villages receive the sun substantially earlier than their valleys receive warmth.  
**How it remains achieved:** An ordered temporal disparity remains established between the two locations.  
**Operative Centre:** The establishment of a temporal precedence through which the mountain villages' earlier encounter with the morning sun becomes intelligible in relation to the valleys' delayed warmth.  
**Diagnostic:** Removing “long before” eliminates the governing temporal precedence.  
**Reading status:** Written

### A-002

**Source ID:** A-002  
**Original sentence:** A river remembers no borders, yet nations argue over its water.  
**What changed:** A tension becomes intelligible between the river's indifference to political boundaries and nations' competing claims.  
**What made it sufficient:** “Yet” establishes opposition between natural continuity and human territorial disagreement.  
**How it remains achieved:** Political divisions govern disputes over water that does not itself recognise those divisions.  
**Operative Centre:** The establishment of an opposition between the river's boundary-independent existence and the politically divided claims made upon its water.  
**Diagnostic:** Replacing “yet” with “and” weakens the governing opposition.  
**Reading status:** Written

### A-003

**Source ID:** A-003  
**Original sentence:** Every forest floor is a crowded marketplace of decay, nourishment, and renewal.  
**What changed:** The forest floor becomes intelligible as an interconnected field of processes.  
**What made it sufficient:** The marketplace metaphor organises decay, nourishment, and renewal into one interdependent ecological scene.  
**How it remains achieved:** A unified field of distinct contributing ecological processes remains intelligible.  
**Operative Centre:** The metaphorical integration of decay, nourishment, and renewal into one intelligible organisation of the forest floor.  
**Diagnostic:** Replacing “marketplace” with “location” loses the interdependence. Alternative: relations among processes rather than their integrated representation; the predication favours integration.  
**Reading status:** Written

### A-004

**Source ID:** A-004  
**Original sentence:** Somewhere tonight, a child will learn the moon follows no one home.  
**What changed:** An anticipated correction becomes intelligible: the moon's apparent accompaniment is not personal following.  
**What made it sufficient:** The contrast between seeming to follow someone and following no one constitutes the anticipated discovery.  
**How it remains achieved:** The sentence establishes the expectation of future learning, not its completed occurrence.  
**Operative Centre:** The intelligible anticipation of a child's discovery that the moon's apparent personal following is not an actual relationship of accompaniment.  
**Diagnostic:** Changing “will learn” to “has learned” changes anticipation into a claimed completed discovery.  
**Reading status:** Written

### A-005

**Source ID:** A-005  
**Original sentence:** Cities grow by making strangers useful to one another without introductions.  
**What changed:** Urban growth becomes intelligible through cooperation without personal familiarity.  
**What made it sufficient:** “Grow by making” establishes impersonal mutual usefulness as the means of growth.  
**How it remains achieved:** An explanatory dependence between urban growth and strangers' coordination remains established.  
**Operative Centre:** The establishment of impersonal mutual usefulness as a governing means of urban growth.  
**Diagnostic:** Removing “by” destroys the explicit means–outcome link; removing “without introductions” loses the impersonal feature.  
**Reading status:** Written

### A-007

### A-008

**Source ID:** A-008

### A-010

**Source ID:** A-010  
**Original sentence:** A grandmother's recipe survives because somebody remembers what cannot be written down.  
**What changed:** A recipe's survival becomes explicable through knowledge exceeding written instructions.  
**What made it sufficient:** “Because” establishes remembrance of unwritable knowledge as the sustaining condition.  
**How it remains achieved:** Continuity remains grounded in someone's remembered or tacit knowledge.  
**Operative Centre:** The establishment of embodied or remembered knowledge as the sustaining condition through which a recipe survives beyond what can be written.  
**Diagnostic:** Replacing “what cannot be written down” with “the written instructions” loses tacit transmission.  
**Reading status:** Written
