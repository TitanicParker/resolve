# Resolve — Canonical Reverse-Card Teaching Standard (v1)
 
## Purpose
Every reverse card teaches how the **resolved structure is identifiable in the exact sentence**. It is an evidence-led explanation written **after**, never before, the canonical two-gate Operative Centre investigation. The frozen Gate One Centre record must not be rewritten to suit a cell. Classification is derived from that frozen Centre only.
 
## Standard lesson (three connected paragraphs)
 
1. **Identification** — State the achievement in ordinary language; quote the minimal decisive words and explain what each contributes. Do not confuse grammatical subject or subject matter with the sentence's governing determination.
2. **Completion** — Describe how the essential contributions meet in one sufficient achievement, locating the sufficiency and distinguishing the nearest serious competing reading. Preserve modality, quantification, negation, scope and dependencies.
3. **Verification** — State positively **what remains achieved**, why it holds, and which precise deletion or alteration would change or defeat it. When classified, explain the three coordinates **as one conjunction**, not as three sequential steps.
 
Display in the card footer: **Transformation Pattern × Completion Topology × Persistence Mode**, canonical cell number and name, research status, and a link to the dated source record. For contested cases preserve all surviving rivals, without choosing a false winner.
 
### Authoring template
**Title:** [Plain-language name of achieved determination]
 
**Identification:** “The sentence establishes [the governing determination] through ‘[exact words]’. [Explain the work the quoted language does.]”
 
**Completion:** “The sentence becomes sufficient when [the indispensable contributions and their relationship]. [Explain how they complete the governing achievement and distinguish the nearest plausible competing account.]”
 
**Verification:** “What remains achieved is [the complete Operative Centre]. If [decisive word/relation] were removed or changed, [precise loss or alteration]. [Where evidenced: one sentence explaining the single Transformation × Topology × Persistence conjunction.]”
 
These are prompts, **not mandatory stock phrases**; use genuinely natural prose grounded in the sentence and frozen research record.
 
## Length and layout
- Compact: **90–130 words** of explanation for transparently resolved sentences.
- Standard: **130–190 words** for most records.
- Extended: **190–260 words** where indispensable complexity warrants it.
- Target around **150–190 words** and use most of the reverse face; keep body type legible (approximately **15–16 px minimum on mobile**) and permit scrolling on exceptional cards rather than compressing arbitrarily.
- Keep the exact source sentence available above the lesson or immediately via the front face.
 
## Truthfulness and workflow
- **Candidate, revised, contested, abstained, and not investigated** remain distinct states. A recorded first-pass candidate is not a uniquely proven classification.
- **Not investigated:** say so; never generate a plausible explanation merely to fill the back.
- **Contested:** show established Centre, the live rival cells and what remains undecided; do not label as settled.
- **Revised:** preserve earlier record and link the current dated revision.
- Research receipts live in Everything Fresh; teaching material and this library reflect them **only after verified publication**. Never treat the number of available cards as the number of resolved sentences.
- Each teaching entry should cite its exact corpus ID and versioned research receipt.
- An LLM improving at the task means **better tested reproducibility, precision, rival discrimination and preservation of uncertainty**; it does not mean that a previous output silently becomes ground truth.
 
**Editorial acceptance test:** After reading the reverse, can someone return to the exact sentence, point to why this achievement is there, and explain why its closest alternative is worse or still unresolved?
